# Risk Assessment Guide

A risk assessment is a structured process for identifying, analyzing, and evaluating risks that could affect an organization's objectives.

This guide provides a simple approach that can be adapted to different organizations and risk-management frameworks.

## 1. Define the Scope

Before beginning an assessment, determine:

* What is being assessed?
* Which business unit is involved?
* Which processes or systems are included?
* What objectives could be affected?
* What assessment period is being considered?

A clearly defined scope helps keep assessments consistent.

## 2. Identify Risks

Identify events or conditions that could prevent objectives from being achieved.

Consider:

* People
* Processes
* Technology
* Suppliers
* Regulations
* Market conditions
* Financial conditions
* Cybersecurity threats
* Operational dependencies
* Strategic changes

A useful risk statement can describe:

**Cause → Risk Event → Consequence**

For example:

> Because a critical application has inadequate access controls, unauthorized users could gain access to sensitive information, resulting in regulatory, financial, and reputational consequences.

## 3. Identify Causes

Determine why the risk could occur.

Potential causes might include:

* Weak controls
* Human error
* System failures
* Third-party dependencies
* Process deficiencies
* External events
* Regulatory changes

## 4. Identify Consequences

Determine what could happen if the risk occurs.

Consequences can include:

* Financial loss
* Operational disruption
* Data loss
* Regulatory penalties
* Customer impact
* Reputational damage
* Safety consequences
* Strategic impact

## 5. Assess Likelihood

Assign a likelihood score using your organization's defined methodology.

A simple 1–5 scale can be used:

| Score | Likelihood     |
| ----: | -------------- |
|     1 | Rare           |
|     2 | Unlikely       |
|     3 | Possible       |
|     4 | Likely         |
|     5 | Almost Certain |

## 6. Assess Impact

Assign an impact score.

| Score | Impact        |
| ----: | ------------- |
|     1 | Insignificant |
|     2 | Minor         |
|     3 | Moderate      |
|     4 | Major         |
|     5 | Severe        |

## 7. Calculate Risk Score

The basic calculation is:

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

Example:

`4 × 5 = 20`

The result should then be compared against the organization's risk-rating thresholds.

## 8. Consider Existing Controls

Identify the controls already in place.

Examples include:

* Policies
* Procedures
* Access controls
* Monitoring
* Backups
* Training
* Contracts
* Insurance
* Technical safeguards

Controls can influence the likelihood, impact, or both.

## 9. Assess Residual Risk

After considering controls, determine the remaining level of risk.

This is commonly called **residual risk**.

## 10. Assign a Risk Owner

Each significant risk should have an accountable owner.

The risk owner should have sufficient authority and responsibility to coordinate the management of the risk.

## 11. Define Treatment Actions

If additional action is required, document:

* Treatment strategy
* Actions
* Responsible person
* Target date
* Required resources
* Expected reduction in risk

## 12. Monitor and Review

Risk assessments should not be considered one-time exercises.

Review risks when:

* The business changes
* New threats emerge
* Regulations change
* Controls change
* Incidents occur
* Risk appetite changes
* Significant projects are launched
