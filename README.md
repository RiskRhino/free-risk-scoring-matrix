## What Is a Risk Scoring Matrix?

A risk scoring matrix is a practical tool used to evaluate and prioritize risks based on two primary factors:

* **Likelihood** — How likely is the risk to occur?
* **Impact** — How serious would the consequences be?

A common approach is to multiply the two values:

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

For example:

> Likelihood: 4 – Likely
> Impact: 5 – Severe
> Risk Score: 4 × 5 = 20

The resulting score can then be used to determine the relative severity of the risk and help decide what action should be taken.

## 5×5 Risk Matrix

The standard 5×5 matrix uses five levels of likelihood and five levels of impact.

### Likelihood Scale

| Score | Likelihood     |
| ----: | -------------- |
|     1 | Rare           |
|     2 | Unlikely       |
|     3 | Possible       |
|     4 | Likely         |
|     5 | Almost Certain |

### Impact Scale

| Score | Impact        |
| ----: | ------------- |
|     1 | Insignificant |
|     2 | Minor         |
|     3 | Moderate      |
|     4 | Major         |
|     5 | Severe        |

Combining these values produces scores from **1 to 25**.

## How to Use a Risk Scoring Matrix

1. **Identify the risk**
   Describe the event or condition that could negatively affect your organization.

2. **Assess likelihood**
   Estimate how likely the risk is to occur.

3. **Assess impact**
   Estimate the potential consequences if the risk occurs.

4. **Calculate the score**
   Multiply likelihood by impact.

5. **Prioritize the risk**
   Compare the resulting score with your organization's risk thresholds.

6. **Determine treatment**
   Decide whether the risk should be accepted, monitored, reduced, transferred, or avoided.

## Inherent Risk and Residual Risk

Risk assessments can distinguish between **inherent risk** and **residual risk**.

### Inherent Risk

The level of risk before existing controls or mitigation measures are considered.

### Residual Risk

The level of risk remaining after controls and mitigation measures have been taken into account.

For example, a risk might initially have a score of 20. After effective controls are implemented, its residual risk might decrease to 8.

## Risk Treatment

A risk score should support a decision about what happens next.

Common risk treatment strategies include:

* **Avoid** — Stop or change the activity creating the risk.
* **Reduce** — Implement controls to reduce likelihood or impact.
* **Transfer** — Transfer some of the risk through contracts, insurance, outsourcing, or other arrangements.
* **Accept** — Formally accept the risk when it falls within the organization's risk appetite.

## Why Use a Risk Matrix?

A risk matrix makes risk information easier to understand and communicate.

It can help teams:

* Compare risks consistently
* Identify high-priority risks
* Communicate risk exposure
* Support management decisions
* Establish treatment priorities
* Monitor changes in risk exposure
* Create a consistent risk assessment methodology

## Important Note

A risk score is not an absolute measurement of risk. Likelihood and impact assessments involve judgement, and different organizations may use different scoring definitions and thresholds.

Your organization should define its own risk appetite, scoring methodology, rating thresholds, and treatment requirements.
