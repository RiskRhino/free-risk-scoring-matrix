# Risk Treatment

Risk treatment is the process of deciding how an organization will respond to an identified risk.

After assessing a risk, an organization should determine whether the risk is acceptable and whether additional action is required.

## Common Risk Treatment Strategies

Four commonly used approaches are:

1. Avoid
2. Reduce
3. Transfer
4. Accept

## 1. Avoid

Risk avoidance means changing or stopping an activity so that the risk no longer applies or is substantially reduced.

Example:

An organization decides not to launch a product in a market because the associated regulatory exposure is unacceptable.

## 2. Reduce

Risk reduction means implementing controls or other measures to reduce likelihood, impact, or both.

Examples include:

* Security controls
* Staff training
* Process improvements
* Redundancy
* Monitoring
* Business continuity measures
* Supplier diversification

## 3. Transfer

Risk transfer involves sharing or transferring some of the financial or operational consequences to another party.

Examples can include:

* Insurance
* Contracts
* Outsourcing
* Service agreements

Risk transfer does not necessarily eliminate the underlying risk.

## 4. Accept

Risk acceptance means consciously deciding to retain a risk.

Acceptance should generally be based on the organization's risk appetite and approval requirements.

A risk should not be considered accepted simply because nobody has taken action.

## Risk Treatment Plan

A treatment plan can contain:

| Field         | Example             |
| ------------- | ------------------- |
| Risk ID       | R-001               |
| Risk          | Unauthorized access |
| Current Score | 20                  |

| Owner         | Security Manager    |
| Due Date      | 2026-12-31          |
| Target Score  | 10                  |
| Status        | In Progress         |

## Prioritizing Treatment

Not every risk requires the same level of action.

Organizations may prioritize risks based on:

* Risk score
* Risk appetite
* Regulatory requirements
* Potential financial impact
* Customer impact
* Strategic importance
* Risk velocity
* Control weaknesses

## Risk Acceptance

A formal risk acceptance process can specify:

* Who can accept risks
* Maximum score that can be accepted
* Required documentation
* Expiration date
* Review frequency
* Escalation requirements

## Monitor Treatment Actions

Treatment actions should be tracked until completion.

After implementation, reassess the risk to determine whether the expected reduction has occurred.

## Example

Initial assessment:

`Likelihood 4 × Impact 5 = 20`

Treatment:

* Implement MFA
* Improve access reviews
* Increase monitoring

New assessment:

`Likelihood 2 × Impact 5 = 10`

The organization can then determine whether a residual score of 10 falls within its acceptable risk level.
